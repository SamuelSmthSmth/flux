### **Question 7**

In a triangle $ABC$, side $AB$ has length $10\text{ cm}$, side $AC$ has length $5\text{ cm}$, and angle $BAC = \theta$ where $\theta$ is measured in degrees. The area of triangle $ABC$ is $15\text{ cm}^2$.

**(a)** Find the two possible values of $\cos \theta$. **(4)**

Given that $BC$ is the longest side of the triangle,

**(b)** find the exact length of $BC$. **(2)**

**(Total for Question 7 is 6 marks)**

### **Mark Scheme 7**

#### **Part (a)**

**Step 1: Use the area formula**

$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}ab \sin C \implies 15 = \frac{1}{2}(10)(5)\sin \theta$$
$$15 = 25\sin \theta \implies \sin \theta = \frac{15}{25} = \frac{3}{5}$$

- **[M1]** Uses the correct area formula $\frac{1}{2}bc\sin A$.
- **[A1]** Correct value for $\sin \theta = \frac{3}{5}$.

**Step 2: Find $\cos \theta$ using trigonometric identities**

$$\cos^2 \theta = 1 - \sin^2 \theta = 1 - \left(\frac{3}{5}\right)^2 = 1 - \frac{9}{25} = \frac{16}{25}$$
$$\cos \theta = \pm \frac{4}{5}$$

- **[M1]** Uses $\cos^2 \theta = 1 - \sin^2 \theta$ or a right-angled triangle method to find $\cos \theta$.
- **[A1]** Both values $\cos \theta = \frac{4}{5}$ and $\cos \theta = -\frac{4}{5}$ found.

#### **Part (b)**

**Step 3: Select the correct $\cos \theta$ for the longest side**

Since $BC$ is the longest side, angle $BAC$ ($\theta$) must be obtuse, which means $\cos \theta$ must be negative. Therefore, $\cos \theta = -\frac{4}{5}$.

- **[M1]** Identifies that $\cos \theta = -\frac{4}{5}$ because $BC$ is the longest side (angle is obtuse).

**Step 4: Apply the cosine rule**

$$BC^2 = 10^2 + 5^2 - 2(10)(5)\left(-\frac{4}{5}\right)$$
$$BC^2 = 100 + 25 - 100\left(-\frac{4}{5}\right) = 125 + 80 = 205$$
$$BC = \sqrt{205}$$

- **[A1]** Exact length $BC = \sqrt{205}$.

#### **Part (b)**

**Step 4: Find the second derivative and test**

$$\frac{d^2C}{dv^2} = \frac{3000}{v^3}$$
When $v = \sqrt{8250}$, $\frac{d^2C}{dv^2} = \frac{3000}{(\sqrt{8250})^3} > 0$, therefore it is a minimum.

### **Question 15**

**Figure 4** _(A sketch of part of the curve $C$ and normal line $l$, showing region $R$ bounded by $x = 2$, curve $C$, normal $l$, and the x-axis)._

Figure 4 shows a sketch of part of the curve $C$ with equation

The line $l$ is the normal to $C$ at the point $P$.

The region $R$, shown shaded in Figure 4, is bounded by the line $l$, the curve $C$, the line with equation $x = 2$ and the $x$-axis.

Show that the area of $R$ is $46$.

#### **Part (a)**
