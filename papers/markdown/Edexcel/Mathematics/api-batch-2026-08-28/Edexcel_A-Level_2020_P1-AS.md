### **Question 1**

Find
$$\int \left( \frac{2}{3}x^3 - 6\sqrt{x} + 1 \right) dx$$
giving your answer in its simplest form. **(4)**

### **Mark Scheme 1**

#### **Part (a)**

**Step 1: Integrate the expression**
$$\int \left( \frac{2}{3}x^3 - 6x^{\frac{1}{2}} + 1 \right) dx = \frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{x^4}{4} - 6 \cdot \frac{x^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\frac{3}{2}} + x + C$$

- **[M1]** Attempt to integrate at least two terms of the expression (power increased by 1).
- **[A1]** Correct integration of the first term: $\frac{1}{6}x^4$.
- **[A1]** Correct integration of the second term: $-4x^{\frac{3}{2}}$.
- **[A1]** Correct integration of the final term and constant: $+x + C$.

### **Question 2**

**(i)** Show that $x^2 - 8x + 17 > 0$ for all real values of $x$. **(3)**

**(ii)** “If I add 3 to a number and square the sum, the result is greater than the square of the original number.”
State, giving a reason, if the above statement is always true, sometimes true or never true. **(2)**

**(Total 5 marks)**

### **Mark Scheme 2**

#### **Part (i)**

**Step 1: Complete the square**
$$x^2 - 8x + 17 = (x - 4)^2 - 16 + 17 = (x - 4)^2 + 1$$

- **[M1]** Attempt to complete the square.
- **[A1]** Correct completion of the square: $(x - 4)^2 + 1$.

**Step 2: Justify the inequality**
Since $(x - 4)^2 \ge 0$ for all $x$, then $(x - 4)^2 + 1 \ge 1 > 0$.

- **[A1]** Correct reasoning and conclusion.

#### **Part (ii)**

**Step 3: Formulate the inequality**
$$(x + 3)^2 > x^2$$
$$x^2 + 6x + 9 > x^2$$
$$6x + 9 > 0$$
$$x > -1.5$$

- **[M1]** Correct expansion and simplification.

**Step 4: State the conclusion**
The statement is sometimes true, as it only holds for $x > -1.5$.

- **[A1]** Correct conclusion with reason.

### **Examiner Report 2**

Candidates found the first part of this question challenging, but they were more successful with the second part, with many fully correct responses. There were a number of common mistakes in candidates’ approaches to part (i). Many candidates tried to find the resultant vector by adding the two given vectors, rather than subtracting them. Secondly, candidates seemed unclear on exactly what angle the question was looking for, with lots of time being spent on finding various different (but unfortunately not relevant) angles in their diagrams. Finally, some candidates took a rather long-winded approach to find the correct angles (through use of the cosine rule to find one part of the required answer, then additional work using tan) as they had not spotted the more straightforward use of $\arctan \frac{7}{3}$. Even those who found a relevant angle were often unable to proceed to find a bearing.

### **Question 3**

Given that the point $A$ has position vector $4\mathbf{i} - 5\mathbf{j}$ and the point $B$ has position vector $-5\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j}$,

**(a)** find the vector $\vec{AB}$. **(2)**

**(b)** find $|\vec{AB}|$.
Give your answer as a simplified surd. **(2)**

**(Total 4 marks)**

### **Mark Scheme 3**

#### **Part (a)**

**Step 1: Calculate the vector**
$$\vec{AB} = \vec{OB} - \vec{OA} = (-5\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j}) - (4\mathbf{i} - 5\mathbf{j})$$
$$\vec{AB} = -9\mathbf{i} + 3\mathbf{j}$$

- **[M1]** Attempt to subtract vectors.
- **[A1]** Correct vector.

#### **Part (b)**

**Step 2: Calculate the magnitude**
$$|\vec{AB}| = \sqrt{(-9)^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{81 + 9} = \sqrt{90}$$
$$|\vec{AB}| = 3\sqrt{10}$$

- **[M1]** Correct use of Pythagoras.
- **[A1]** Correct simplified surd.

### **Question 5**

A student’s attempt to solve the equation $2\log_2 x - \log_2 \sqrt{x} = 3$ is shown below.

$$2\log_2 x - \log_2 \sqrt{x} = 3$$
$$2\log_2 \left( \frac{x}{\sqrt{x}} \right) = 3 \quad \text{using the subtraction law for logs}$$
$$2\log_2 (\sqrt{x}) = 3 \quad \text{simplifying}$$
$$\log_2 x = 3 \quad \text{using the power law for logs}$$
$$x = 3^2 = 9 \quad \text{using the definition of a log}$$

**(a)** Identify two errors made by this student, giving a brief explanation of each. **(2)**

**(b)** Write out the correct solution. **(3)**

**(Total 5 marks)**

### **Mark Scheme 5**

#### **Part (a)**

**Step 1: Identify error 1**
The student incorrectly applied the subtraction law for logs. The coefficient 2 should have been handled before subtracting.

- **[B1]** Correct identification and explanation.

**Step 2: Identify error 2**
The student used the definition of a log incorrectly at the end ($x = 3^2$ instead of $x = 2^3$).

- **[B1]** Correct identification and explanation.

#### **Part (b)**

**Step 3: Correct solution**
$$2\log_2 x - \log_2 x^{\frac{1}{2}} = 3$$
$$\log_2 x^2 - \log_2 x^{\frac{1}{2}} = 3$$
$$\log_2 \left( \frac{x^2}{x^{\frac{1}{2}}} \right) = 3$$
$$\log_2 x^{\frac{3}{2}} = 3$$
$$x^{\frac{3}{2}} = 2^3 = 8$$
$$x = 8^{\frac{2}{3}} = 4$$

- **[M1]** Correct use of log laws to combine terms.
- **[M1]** Correct conversion to index form.
- **[A1]** Correct final answer $x = 4$.

### **Question 7**

In a triangle $ABC$, side $AB$ has length $10\text{ cm}$, side $AC$ has length $5\text{ cm}$, and angle $BAC = \theta$, where $\theta$ is measured in degrees. The area of triangle $ABC$ is $15\text{ cm}^2$.

**(a)** Find the two possible values of $\cos \theta$. **(4)**

**(b)** find the exact length of $BC$. **(2)**

**(Total 6 marks)**

### **Mark Scheme 7**

**Step 1: Use area formula**
$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}ab \sin \theta = 15$$
$$\frac{1}{2} \cdot 10 \cdot 5 \cdot \sin \theta = 15$$

- **[M1]** Correct use of area formula.

$$\cos \theta = \pm 0.8$$

- **[M1]** Use $\cos^2 \theta + \sin^2 \theta = 1$.
- **[A1]** Both values correct.

#### **Part (b)**

**Step 3: Use cosine rule**
$$BC^2 = 10^2 + 5^2 - 2(10)(5)\cos \theta$$
Since $BC$ is the longest side, $\theta$ must be obtuse, so $\cos \theta = -0.8$.
$$BC^2 = 100 + 25 - 100(-0.8) = 125 + 80 = 205$$
$$BC = \sqrt{205}$$

- **[M1]** Correct use of cosine rule.

### **Question 8**

A lorry is driven between London and Newcastle.
In a simple model, the cost of the journey $£C$ when the lorry is driven at a steady speed of $v$ kilometres per hour is
$$C = \frac{1500}{v} + \frac{2}{11}v + 60$$

**(a)** Find, according to this model,
**(i)** the value of $v$ that minimises the cost of the journey,
**(ii)** the minimum cost of the journey.
(Solutions based entirely on graphical or numerical methods are not acceptable.) **(6)**

**(b)** Prove by using $\frac{d^2C}{dv^2}$ that the cost is minimised at the speed found in (a)(i). **(2)**

**(c)** State one limitation of this model. **(1)**

**(Total 9 marks)**

### **Mark Scheme 8**

#### **Part (a)**

**Step 1: Differentiate**
$$\frac{dC}{dv} = -1500v^{-2} + \frac{2}{11}$$

- **[M1]** Attempt to differentiate.
- **[A1]** Correct derivative.

$$v^2 = \frac{1500 \cdot 11}{2} = 8250 \Rightarrow v = \sqrt{8250} \approx 90.8$$

- **[M1]** Set derivative to 0 and solve for $v$.

**Step 3: Find minimum cost**
$$C = \frac{1500}{90.829} + \frac{2}{11}(90.829) + 60 \approx 16.51 + 16.51 + 60 = 93.02$$

- **[A1]** Correct minimum cost.

#### **Part (b)**

**Step 4: Second derivative test**
$$\frac{d^2C}{dv^2} = 3000v^{-3}$$
At $v \approx 90.8$, $\frac{d^2C}{dv^2} > 0$, therefore it is a minimum.

- **[M1]** Find second derivative.
- **[A1]** Correct conclusion.

#### **Part (c)**

**Step 5: Limitation**
Speed is assumed to be constant; traffic/road conditions are not considered.

- **[B1]** Correct limitation.

As $h \to 0$, $3x^2 + 3xh + h^2 \to 3x^2$.

### **Mark Scheme 15**

**Step 1: Find the normal**
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = -64x^{-3} + 3$$
At $x=4$, $\frac{dy}{dx} = -64(64)^{-1} + 3 = -1 + 3 = 2$.
Gradient of normal $m = -1/2$.
Equation: $y - 6 = -1/2(x - 4) \Rightarrow y = -0.5x + 8$.

- **[M1]** Differentiate.
- **[A1]** Gradient of tangent.
- **[M1]** Gradient of normal.
- **[A1]** Equation of normal.

**Step 2: Integrate curve**
$$\int_2^4 \left( 32x^{-2} + 3x - 8 \right) dx = [-32x^{-1} + 1.5x^2 - 8x]_2^4$$
$$= (-8 + 24 - 32) - (-16 + 6 - 16) = -16 - (-26) = 10$$

- **[M1]** Integrate curve.
- **[A1]** Correct integral.
- **[A1]** Correct evaluation.

Area under normal from $x=2$ to $x=4$:
$$\int_2^4 (-0.5x + 8) dx = [-0.25x^2 + 8x]_2^4 = (-4 + 32) - (-1 + 16) = 28 - 15 = 13$$
