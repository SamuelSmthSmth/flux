### **Question 1**

A car moves in a straight line along a horizontal road with constant acceleration $2\text{ms}^{-2}$

The car is moving with speed $15\text{ms}^{-1}$ in the direction of the acceleration when it passes a signpost on the road.

The car is modelled as a particle.

**(a)** Use the model to find the speed of the car $4\text{s}$ after passing the signpost. **(2)**

Figure 1 below shows the horizontal forces acting on the car.

Given that
* the car has mass $800\text{ kg}$
* the driving force of the engine has magnitude $D\text{ newtons}$
* the resistance to the motion of the car has magnitude $400\text{ N}$
* the acceleration of the car is $2\text{ms}^{-2}$ in the direction of the driving force

**(b)** use the model to find the value of $D$. **(2)**

**Figure 1** _(Horizontal forces acting on a car of mass $800\text{ kg}$ with acceleration $2\text{ms}^{-2}$, resistance $400\text{ N}$ to the left and driving force $D\text{ N}$ to the right)_

**(Total for Question 1 is 4 marks)**

### **Mark Scheme 1**

#### **Part (a)**

**Step 1: Use suvat equation to find final velocity**
$$v = u + at \Rightarrow v = 15 + (2)(4)$$
- **[M1]** Uses a correct suvat formula with $u = 15$, $a = 2$, $t = 4$.
$$v = 23\text{ ms}^{-1}$$
- **[A1]** Correct speed.

#### **Part (b)**

**Step 2: Apply Newton's Second Law**
$$D - 400 = 800 \times 2$$
- **[M1]** Forms a correct equation of motion using $F = ma$.
$$D = 2000$$
- **[A1]** Correct value for $D$.

### **Question 2**

A small box $B$ of mass $2\text{ kg}$ is dragged in a straight line, along a rough horizontal plane, at a constant speed by a force of magnitude $5\text{N}$.

The line of action of the force makes an angle $\alpha$ with the plane, where $\sin\alpha = \frac{3}{5}$, as shown in Figure 2.

**(a)** Show that the magnitude of the normal reaction of the plane on the box is $16.6\text{ N}$. **(3)**

At the instant when $B$ is at the point $O$ on the plane, the force of magnitude $5\text{ N}$ is removed.

**(b)** Describe the motion of the box after the force of magnitude $5\text{ N}$ is removed. **(1)**

**(c)** Find the magnitude of the normal reaction of the plane on the box after the force of magnitude $5\text{ N}$ is removed. **(1)**

Given that after the force of magnitude $5\text{ N}$ is removed
* the box is modelled as a particle
* air resistance is modelled as being negligible
* the coefficient of friction between the box and the plane is modelled as $0.2$
* the speed of the box as it passes through $O$ is $4\text{ ms}^{-1}$
* the box comes to rest at the point $X$ on the plane

**(d)** use the model to find the length $OX$. **(4)**

**(e)** State one limitation of the model, apart from ignoring air resistance, that could affect your answer to part (d). **(1)**

**Figure 2** _(A box $B$ of mass $2\text{ kg}$ on a horizontal plane pulled by a force of $5\text{ N}$ at an angle $\alpha$ to the plane)_

**(Total for Question 2 is 10 marks)**

### **Mark Scheme 2**

#### **Part (a)**

**Step 1: Resolve forces vertically**
$$R + 5\sin\alpha = 2g$$
$$R + 5\left(\frac{3}{5}\right) = 2(9.8)$$
$$R + 3 = 19.6 \Rightarrow R = 16.6\text{ N}$$
- **[B1]** Correct vertical resolution identifying all three vertical forces.
- **[M1]** Substitutes $\sin\alpha = \frac{3}{5}$ and $g = 9.8$.
- **[A1]** Obtains the printed result $16.6\text{ N}$ with no errors.

#### **Part (b)**

**Step 2: Describe the motion**
Decelerating to a stop (or slowing down).
- **[B1]** Correct description.

#### **Part (c)**

**Step 3: Find new normal reaction**
$$R = 2g = 19.6\text{ N}$$
- **[B1]** Correct value for the normal reaction.

#### **Part (d)**

**Step 4: Find the frictional force**
$$F_{\text{max}} = \mu R = 0.2 \times 19.6 = 3.92\text{ N}$$
- **[M1]** Uses $F = \mu R$ with their reaction from part (c).

**Step 5: Apply Newton's Second Law**
$$-3.92 = 2a \Rightarrow a = -1.96\text{ ms}^{-2}$$
- **[M1]** Uses $F = ma$ to find the acceleration.

**Step 6: Use suvat to find distance $OX$**
$$v^2 = u^2 + 2as \Rightarrow 0 = 4^2 + 2(-1.96)s$$
$$3.92s = 16 \Rightarrow s = 4.08\dots$$
$$OX = 4.1\text{ m}\text{ (or } 4.08\text{ m)}$$
- **[dM1]** Dependent on previous method marks, uses a correct suvat equation to find $s$.
- **[A1]** Correct distance to 2 or 3 significant figures.

#### **Part (e)**

**Step 7: State a limitation**
e.g., The box is not a particle (has size/dimensions), or the coefficient of friction may vary, or the plane may not be perfectly smooth/flat.
- **[B1]** Any valid limitation related to the assumptions of the model.

### **Question 3**

[In this question $\mathbf{i}$ and $\mathbf{j}$ are horizontal unit vectors due east and due north respectively.]

A particle $P$ of mass $0.5\text{ kg}$ moves with constant acceleration $(2\mathbf{i} - 2.4\mathbf{j})\text{ms}^{-2}$ on a smooth horizontal plane under the action of a constant horizontal force $\mathbf{F}\text{ N}$.

**(a)** Find $\mathbf{F}$ in terms of $\mathbf{i}$ and $\mathbf{j}$. **(1)**

At time $t = 0$, $P$ is moving with velocity $(-7\mathbf{i} + 7.8\mathbf{j})\text{ms}^{-1}$

**(b)** Find the velocity of $P$ at time $t = 2\text{ seconds}$. **(2)**

**(c)** Find the direction of motion of $P$ at time $t = 2\text{ seconds}$, giving your answer as a bearing in degrees. **(3)**

At time $t = 0$, $P$ passes through the point $O$.

At time $t = 5\text{ seconds}$, $P$ passes through the point $A$.

**(d)** Find $\vec{OA}$ in terms of $\mathbf{i}$ and $\mathbf{j}$. **(2)**

**(Total for Question 3 is 8 marks)**

### **Mark Scheme 3**

#### **Part (a)**

**Step 1: Calculate force $\mathbf{F}$**
$$\mathbf{F} = m\mathbf{a} = 0.5(2\mathbf{i} - 2.4\mathbf{j}) = \mathbf{i} - 1.2\mathbf{j}$$
- **[B1]** Correct vector for $\mathbf{F}$.

#### **Part (b)**

**Step 2: Find velocity at $t = 2$**
$$\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{u} + \mathbf{a}t = (-7\mathbf{i} + 7.8\mathbf{j}) + (2\mathbf{i} - 2.4\mathbf{j})(2)$$
$$\mathbf{v} = -7\mathbf{i} + 7.8\mathbf{j} + 4\mathbf{i} - 4.8\mathbf{j} = -3\mathbf{i} + 3\mathbf{j}$$
- **[M1]** Uses $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{u} + \mathbf{a}t$ with correct substitution.
- **[A1]** Correct velocity vector.

#### **Part (c)**

**Step 3: Find the direction of motion**
$$\tan\theta = \frac{3}{|-3|} = 1 \Rightarrow \theta = 45^\circ\text{ to the west of north}$$
$$\text{Bearing} = 360^\circ - 45^\circ = 315^\circ$$
- **[M1]** Correct use of trigonometry to find the angle with respect to the coordinate axes.
- **[M1]** Correct method to convert the angle into a valid compass bearing.
- **[A1]** Correct bearing of $315^\circ$.

#### **Part (d)**

**Step 4: Find displacement vector $\vec{OA}$**
$$\vec{s} = \mathbf{u}t + \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}t^2 = (-7\mathbf{i} + 7.8\mathbf{j})(5) + \frac{1}{2}(2\mathbf{i} - 2.4\mathbf{j})(5^2)$$
$$\vec{s} = -35\mathbf{i} + 39\mathbf{j} + 25\mathbf{i} - 30\mathbf{j} = -10\mathbf{i} + 9\mathbf{j}$$
- **[M1]** Uses a correct suvat equation for displacement with $t = 5$.
- **[A1]** Correct position vector $\vec{OA}$.

### **Question 4**

[In this question, position vectors are given relative to a fixed origin $O$.]

At time $t$ seconds, where $t > 0$, the position vector of a particle $P$ is $\mathbf{r}$ metres where
$$\mathbf{r} = 4t^{\frac{3}{2}}\mathbf{i} - t^2\mathbf{j}$$

**(a)** Find the position vector of $P$ at $t = 4$. **(1)**

**(b)** Find the exact distance of $P$ from $O$ at $t = 4$. **(2)**

**(c)** Find an expression for the velocity of $P$ at time $t$ seconds, where $t > 0$, giving your answer in terms of $t$, $\mathbf{i}$ and $\mathbf{j}$. **(2)**

At $t = T$, the acceleration of $P$ is in a direction that is perpendicular to the line with equation $y = \frac{1}{3}x$.

**(d)** Find the value of $T$. **(4)**

**(Total for Question 4 is 9 marks)**

### **Mark Scheme 4**

#### **Part (a)**

**Step 1: Substitute $t = 4$ into $\mathbf{r}$**
$$\mathbf{r}(4) = 4(4^{\frac{3}{2}})\mathbf{i} - (4^2)\mathbf{j} = 4(8)\mathbf{i} - 16\mathbf{j} = 32\mathbf{i} - 16\mathbf{j}$$
- **[B1]** Correct position vector.

#### **Part (b)**

**Step 2: Find magnitude of $\mathbf{r}$**
$$\text{Distance} = \sqrt{32^2 + (-16)^2} = \sqrt{1024 + 256} = \sqrt{1280} = 16\sqrt{5}$$
- **[M1]** Applies Pythagoras' theorem to the components of $\mathbf{r}$.
- **[A1]** Correct exact distance $16\sqrt{5}$.

#### **Part (c)**

**Step 3: Differentiate position with respect to $t$**
$$\mathbf{v} = \frac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt} = \frac{3}{2}(4)t^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{i} - 2t\mathbf{j} = 6t^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{i} - 2t\mathbf{j}$$
- **[M1]** Differentiates at least one component correctly with respect to $t$.
- **[A1]** Correct velocity expression in terms of $t$, $\mathbf{i}$ and $\mathbf{j}$.

#### **Part (d)**

**Step 4: Find acceleration vector**
$$\mathbf{a} = \frac{d\mathbf{v}}{dt} = 3t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j}$$
- **[M1]** Differentiates velocity to obtain acceleration.

**Step 5: Use perpendicularity condition**
The line $y = \frac{1}{3}x$ has a gradient of $\frac{1}{3}$.
A perpendicular vector must have a gradient of $-3$.
$$\frac{a_y}{a_x} = -3 \Rightarrow \frac{-2}{3T^{-\frac{1}{2}}} = -3$$
$$\frac{2}{3T^{-\frac{1}{2}}} = 3 \Rightarrow 3\sqrt{T} = \frac{2}{3} \Rightarrow \sqrt{T} = \frac{2}{9}$$
$$T = \frac{4}{81}$$
- **[M1]** Identifies the gradient of the line and sets up the perpendicular gradient relationship.
- **[M1]** Solves for $T$.
- **[A1]** Correct value $T = \frac{4}{81}$.

### **Question 5**

A small stone is projected with speed $14\text{ ms}^{-1}$ from a point $O$ on the top of a cliff. 

The point $O$ is $H$ metres vertically above the point $N$. 

Point $N$ is on horizontal ground.

The stone is projected at an angle $\theta$ above the horizontal, where $\tan\theta = \frac{1}{2}$.

The stone strikes the horizontal ground at the point $A$, where $NA = 40\text{ m}$, as shown in Figure 3.

The stone is modelled as a particle moving freely under gravity.

Using this model, find

**(a)** the value of $H$, **(5)**

**(b)** the maximum height of the stone above the horizontal ground. **(3)**

**Figure 3** _(A stone projected from a cliff at $O$ with speed $14\text{ ms}^{-1}$ at angle $\theta$, landing at $A$ on ground $N$)_

**(Total for Question 5 is 8 marks)**

### **Mark Scheme 5**

#### **Part (a)**

**Step 1: Find horizontal and vertical components of initial velocity**
$$\tan\theta = \frac{1}{2} \Rightarrow \sin\theta = \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}, \quad \cos\theta = \frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}$$
$$u_x = 14\cos\theta = 14\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}\right) = \frac{28}{\sqrt{5}}$$
$$u_y = 14\sin\theta = 14\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\right) = \frac{14}{\sqrt{5}}$$
- **[B1]** Correct trigonometric ratios for $\theta$ or correct initial velocity components.

**Step 2: Find time of flight $t$**
$$\text{Horizontal distance: } x = u_x t \Rightarrow 40 = \left(\frac{28}{\sqrt{5}}\right)t$$
$$t = \frac{40\sqrt{5}}{28} = \frac{10\sqrt{5}}{7}\text{ seconds}$$
- **[M1]** Uses horizontal motion equation to find time in terms of $t$.

**Step 3: Find vertical displacement $H$**
Taking downwards as positive:
$$y = u_y t + \frac{1}{2}gt^2$$
$$H = -\left(\frac{14}{\sqrt{5}}\right)\left(\frac{10\sqrt{5}}{7}\right) + \frac{1}{2}(9.8)\left(\frac{10\sqrt{5}}{7}\right)^2$$
$$H = -20 + \frac{4.9(500)}{49} = -20 + 50 = 30\text{ m}$$
- **[M1]** Uses vertical motion equation with time from horizontal motion.
- **[A1]** Correct value $H = 30$.

#### **Part (b)**

**Step 4: Find maximum height above ground**
Maximum height above $O$:
$$v_y^2 = u_y^2 + 2as \Rightarrow 0 = \left(\frac{14}{\sqrt{5}}\right)^2 - 2(9.8)h_{\text{max}}$$
$$0 = \frac{196}{5} - 19.6h_{\text{max}} \Rightarrow 39.2 - 19.6h_{\text{max}} = 0 \Rightarrow h_{\text{max}} = 2\text{ m}$$
Total height above ground = $H + h_{\text{max}} = 30 + 2 = 32\text{ m}$.
- **[M1]** Uses a correct suvat equation to find the maximum height above the point of projection $O$.
- **[M1]** Adds $H$ to find total height above the ground.
- **[A1]** Correct maximum height $32\text{ m}$.

### **Question 6**

A uniform rod $AB$ has mass $M$ and length $2a$.

A particle of mass $2M$ is attached to the rod at the point $C$, where $AC = 0.5a$.

The rod rests with end $A$ on rough horizontal ground and end $B$ against a vertical wall.

The rod lies in a vertical plane which is perpendicular to the wall.

The rod is in equilibrium at an angle $\alpha$ to the wall, as shown in Figure 4.

In an initial model
* the vertical wall is modelled as being smooth
* the magnitude of the normal reaction of the ground on the rod at $A$ is $R$
* the magnitude of the force exerted on the rod by the wall at $B$ is $S$

Using the model,

**(a)** find $R$ in terms of $M$ and $g$, **(1)**

**(b)** show that $S = M\tan\alpha$. **(3)**

In a refined model
* the vertical wall is modelled as being rough
* the magnitude of the normal reaction of the ground on the rod at $A$ is $R_1$

**(c)** State which is greater, $R$ or $R_1$, giving a reason for your answer. **(1)**

**Figure 4** _(A rod $AB$ resting on rough ground and against a smooth wall with a particle at $C$)_

**(Total for Question 6 is 5 marks)**

### **Mark Scheme 6**

#### **Part (a)**

**Step 1: Resolve vertically**
Total downward force = weight of rod ($Mg$) + weight of particle ($2Mg$) = $3Mg$.
$$R = 3Mg$$
- **[B1]** Correct expression for $R$.

#### **Part (b)**

**Step 2: Take moments about $A$**
$$\text{Clockwise moments} = \text{Anticlockwise moments}$$
$$Mg(a\sin\alpha) + 2Mg(0.5a\sin\alpha) = S(2a\cos\alpha)$$
$$Mga\sin\alpha + Mga\sin\alpha = 2aS\cos\alpha$$
$$2Mga\sin\alpha = 2aS\cos\alpha \Rightarrow S\cos\alpha = Mg\sin\alpha$$
$$S = Mg\frac{\sin\alpha}{\cos\alpha} = Mg\tan\alpha$$
- **[M1]** Correctly identifies all forces contributing to moments about $A$ with correct distances.
- **[M1]** Forms the correct moments equation equating clockwise and anticlockwise moments.
- **[A1]** Obtains the printed result $S = Mg\tan\alpha$ with no errors.

#### **Part (c)**

**Step 3: Compare $R$ and $R_1$**
$R$ is greater than $R_1$ (or $R_1$ is less than $R$).
Reason: When the wall is rough, the upward frictional force at the wall helps support the vertical weight, reducing the required normal reaction at $A$.
- **[B1]** States $R$ is greater with a valid physical explanation.

### **Examiner Report 6**

#### **General Comments**
The paper proved to be very accessible with the majority of learners able to make good attempts at all six questions. Generally, time was not an apparent issue. Question 1 was the best answered question, whilst question 6 proved to be the most challenging for learners.

#### **Detailed Comments by Question**

**Question 1**
Part (a) proved to be a friendly starter and the vast majority successfully quoted and used the correct suvat equation for final velocity. In part (b), most responses scored both marks by correctly finding the value of the driving force of the engine using $F = ma$. Common errors included subtracting the resistance force instead of adding it, or incorrectly including $g$ with the mass of $800\text{ kg}$.

**Question 2**
Part (a) was generally well done. Successful responses resolved vertically to form an equation with the vertical component of $5\text{ N}$ clearly found. Part (b) was well answered, describing the motion as decelerating to a stop. Part (c) yielded $2g$ or $19.6\text{ N}$ for the reaction. Part (d) required planning: finding friction, applying Newton's second law, and using suvat. The final mark was frequently lost by failing to round correctly to 2 or 3 s.f. Part (e) required references to the model (e.g., the box being modelled as a particle).

**Question 3**
Part (a) was well answered using $\mathbf{F} = m\mathbf{a}$. Part (b) was answered correctly using $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{u} + \mathbf{a}t$. Part (c) proved challenging; while many found the acute angle from the velocity components, converting it to a correct bearing (or $315^\circ$) was frequently missed due to unrepresentative diagrams or misunderstanding of bearings. Part (d) was well answered using either suvat or integration.

**Question 4**
Part (a) and (b) were well done, though some failed to give exact surd forms in part (b). Part (c) was answered well by differentiating to find the velocity vector. Part (d) proved to be a good discriminator: while many could differentiate to find the acceleration vector, correctly identifying the perpendicular gradient corresponding to $y = \frac{1}{3}x$ caused widespread difficulty.

**Question 5**
Part (a) saw many excellent responses using horizontal motion to find time and substituting into the vertical motion equation. Common errors involved sign confusion when defining positive directions or incorrect resolution of velocity components. Part (b) was well done using $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$, though some candidates dropped squares or rounded prematurely.

**Question 6**
Part (a) was straightforward, with most correctly stating $3Mg$. Part (b) required taking moments about $A$. The most common issues arose from using $(90^\circ - \alpha)$ or making algebraic slips when simplifying trigonometric expressions to reach the printed answer. Part (c) was answered less successfully; many recognised $R > R_1$ but failed to provide a clear mechanical explanation involving vertical equilibrium. Part (d) (from the full question set) was omitted or poorly attempted by many due to structural confusion in setting up moments and force resolutions.
