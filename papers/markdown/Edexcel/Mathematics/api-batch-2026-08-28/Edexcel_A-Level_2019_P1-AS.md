### **Question 1**

Find
$$\int \left(\frac{2}{3}x^3 - 6\sqrt{x} + 1\right) dx$$
giving your answer in its simplest form. **(4)**

**(Total for Question 1 is 4 marks)**

### **Mark Scheme 1**

#### **Part (a)**
**Step 1: Express terms with indices ready for integration**
$$\int \left(\frac{2}{3}x^3 - 6x^{\frac{1}{2}} + 1\right) dx$$
- **[B1]** Writes at least two terms with correct index form (implied by correct integration).

**Step 2: Integrate each term**
$$= \frac{2}{3} \left(\frac{x^4}{4}\right) - 6\left(\frac{x^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\frac{3}{2}}\right) + x$$
- **[M1]** Integrates $x^n \to x^{n+1}$, at least one term correct.
- **[A1]** Two terms correctly integrated. (Does not need $C$ yet).

**Step 3: Simplify and add constant of integration**
$$= \frac{1}{6}x^4 - 4x^{\frac{3}{2}} + x + C$$
- **[A1]** Fully correct and simplified expression including $+ C$.
