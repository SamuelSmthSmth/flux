$$t = 5 \ln \left( \frac{75}{t + 5} \right)$$ **(4)**

$$t_{n+1} = 5 \ln \left( \frac{75}{t_n + 5} \right) \quad \text{with } t_1 = 8$$

**(c)** (i) find the value of $t_3$ to 3 decimal places,

(ii) find, by repeated iteration, the time taken for the car to reach maximum speed. **(3)**

### **Mark Scheme 9**

#### **Part (a)**

**Step 1: Set $v = 0$ for $T > 0$**
$$15T - T\mathrm{e}^{0.2T} = 0 \Rightarrow T(15 - \mathrm{e}^{0.2T}) = 0$$
$$\mathrm{e}^{0.2T} = 15 \Rightarrow 0.2T = \ln 15 \Rightarrow T = 5\ln 15 \approx 13.5$$

- **[M1]** Sets $v = 0$ and solves for $T$ using logarithms.

#### **Part (b)**

**Step 2: Differentiate $v$ using the product rule**
$$\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}t} = 15 - \left(1 \cdot \mathrm{e}^{0.2t} + t \cdot 0.2\mathrm{e}^{0.2t}\right) = 15 - \mathrm{e}^{0.2t}(1 + 0.2t)$$

- **[M1]** Differentiates $t\mathrm{e}^{0.2t}$ using product rule.
- **[A1]** Correct derivative $\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}t} = 15 - \mathrm{e}^{0.2t}(1 + 0.2t)$.

**Step 3: Set derivative to 0 and rearrange**
$$15 - \mathrm{e}^{0.2t}(1 + 0.2t) = 0 \Rightarrow \mathrm{e}^{0.2t}(5 + t) = 75$$

- **[M1]** Sets $\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}t} = 0$ and multiplies by 5 to clear fractions in $(1 + 0.2t)$.

$$\mathrm{e}^{0.2t} = \frac{75}{t + 5} \Rightarrow 0.2t = \ln \left( \frac{75}{t + 5} \right) \Rightarrow t = 5\ln \left( \frac{75}{t + 5} \right)$$

- **[A1]** Clear and complete proof to given answer.

#### **Part (c)**

**Step 4: Calculate $t_2$ and $t_3$**
$$t_1 = 8$$
$$t_2 = 5\ln\left(\frac{75}{13}\right) \approx 8.759$$
$$t_3 = 5\ln\left(\frac{75}{8.759 + 5}\right) \approx 8.477$$

- **[M1]** Attempts $t_2$ correctly using formula.
- **[A1]** $t_3 = 8.477$ (to 3 d.p.).

**Step 5: Perform repeated iterations for maximum speed time**

### **Question 10**

Given that

**(a)** find $\vec{PR}$. **(2)**

### **Mark Scheme 10**

#### **Part (a)**

**Step 1: Add vector components**
$$\vec{PR} = \vec{PQ} + \vec{QR} = (2\mathbf{i} + 8\mathbf{j} - 2\mathbf{k}) + (6\mathbf{i} + 6\mathbf{k}) = 8\mathbf{i} + 8\mathbf{j} + 4\mathbf{k}$$

$$|\vec{QR}| = \sqrt{6^2 + 0^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{36 + 0 + 36} = \sqrt{72}$$
$$|\vec{PR}| = \sqrt{8^2 + 8^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{64 + 64 + 16} = \sqrt{144} = 12$$

- **[M1]** Method to find the magnitude of at least two vectors.

$$\text{Therefore, triangle } PQR \text{ is right-angled.}$$

- **[A1]** Shows $|\vec{PQ}|^2 + |\vec{QR}|^2 = |\vec{PR}|^2$ or $\vec{PQ} \cdot \vec{QR} = 0$ with full concluding statement.

### **Question 16**

**Figure 5** shows a sketch of the curve $C$ with equation

$$y = \frac{1}{x^2 \sqrt{4 - x^2}} \quad 0 < x < 2$$

The region $R$, shown shaded in Figure 5, is bounded by $C$, the line with equation $x = 1$, the $x$-axis and the line with equation $x = \sqrt{3}$

**(a)** Use the substitution $x = 2\sin u$ to show that the area of $R$ is given by

**(b)** Hence, using algebraic integration, find the exact area of $R$. Give your answer in simplest form. **(3)**

### **Mark Scheme 16**

#### **Part (a)**

**Step 1: Differentiate substitution and transform integral terms**
$$x = 2\sin u \Rightarrow \mathrm{d}x = 2\cos u \, \mathrm{d}u$$
$$\sqrt{4 - x^2} = \sqrt{4 - 4\sin^2 u} = 2\cos u$$

- **[M1]** Finds $\mathrm{d}x = 2\cos u \, \mathrm{d}u$ and simplifies $\sqrt{4 - x^2}$ to $2\cos u$.

$$\text{When } x = \sqrt{3}, \quad 2\sin u = \sqrt{3} \Rightarrow u = \frac{\pi}{3}$$
