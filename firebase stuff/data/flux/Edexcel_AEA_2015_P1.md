### **Mark Scheme 7**

#### **Part (a)**

**Step 1: Perform the substitution** $x = \sec \theta \Rightarrow dx = \sec \theta \tan \theta d\theta$ Integral becomes: $I = \int \frac{\sec \theta \tan \theta d\theta}{(\sec^2 \theta - 1)^{3/2}}$

- **[M1]** Suitable 1st step: find $dx$ and substitute.
    

**Step 2: Simplify the integrand** $\sec^2 \theta - 1 = \tan^2 \theta \Rightarrow (\tan^2 \theta)^{3/2} = \tan^3 \theta$ $I = \int \frac{\sec \theta \tan \theta}{\tan^3 \theta} d\theta = \int \frac{\sec \theta}{\tan^2 \theta} d\theta = \int \frac{1/\cos \theta}{\sin^2 \theta / \cos^2 \theta} d\theta = \int \frac{\cos \theta}{\sin^2 \theta} d\theta = \int \csc \theta \cot \theta d\theta$

- **[M1, A1]** Correct integration prep; simplifies to $\csc\theta\cot\theta$.
    

**Step 3: Integrate and apply limits** $\int \csc \theta \cot \theta d\theta = -\csc \theta$ Change limits: When $x = 2$, $\sec \theta = 2 \Rightarrow \cos \theta = 1/2 \Rightarrow \theta = \pi/3$. When $x = \sqrt{2}$, $\sec \theta = \sqrt{2} \Rightarrow \cos \theta = 1/\sqrt{2} \Rightarrow \theta = \pi/4$. $I = [-\csc \theta]_{\pi/4}^{\pi/3} = -\csc(\pi/3) - (-\csc(\pi/4)) = -\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} + \sqrt{2}$

- **[M1, A1 cso]** Changing limits and final exact evaluation to the given answer: $\frac{\sqrt{6} - 2}{\sqrt{3}}$.
    

#### **Part (b)**

**Step 4: Integration by parts** Let $J = \int \csc \theta \cot^2 \theta d\theta = \int \cot \theta (\csc \theta \cot \theta) d\theta$. Let $u = \cot \theta \Rightarrow du = -\csc^2 \theta d\theta$. Let $dv = \csc \theta \cot \theta d\theta \Rightarrow v = -\csc \theta$. $J = -\csc \theta \cot \theta - \int (-\csc \theta)(-\csc^2 \theta) d\theta$ $J = -\csc \theta \cot \theta - \int \csc^3 \theta d\theta$

- **[M1, M1]** Attempt integration by parts; completely correct application.
    

**Step 5: Use trigonometric identities** Rewrite $\csc^3 \theta$: $\csc^3 \theta = \csc \theta (\csc^2 \theta) = \csc \theta (1 + \cot^2 \theta) = \csc \theta + \csc \theta \cot^2 \theta$ Substitute back into $J$: $J = -\csc \theta \cot \theta - \int (\csc \theta + \csc \theta \cot^2 \theta) d\theta$ $J = -\csc \theta \cot \theta - \int \csc \theta d\theta - J$

- **[A1, M1]** Uses $1 + \cot^2 \theta$ and recognizes $J$ reappearing.
    

**Step 6: Solve for J** We know $\int \csc \theta d\theta = -\ln|\csc \theta + \cot \theta|$. $2J = -\csc \theta \cot \theta - (-\ln|\csc \theta + \cot \theta|)$ $J = \frac{1}{2} [ \ln|\csc \theta + \cot \theta| - \csc \theta \cot \theta ] + c \quad (*)$

- **[B1, M1, A1 cso]** Uses known integral of $\csc \theta$; isolates $J$ and concludes correctly.
    

#### **Part (c)**

**Step 7: Setup Volume Integral** $V = \pi \int_{\sqrt{2}}^{2} y^2 dx = \pi \int_{\sqrt{2}}^{2} \frac{1}{(x^2 - 1)^3} dx$

- **[B1]** Correct integral including limits.
    

**Step 8: Substitute and simplify** Using $x = \sec \theta$ as before: $V = \pi \int_{\pi/4}^{\pi/3} \frac{\sec \theta \tan \theta}{\tan^6 \theta} d\theta = \pi \int_{\pi/4}^{\pi/3} \frac{\sec \theta}{\tan^5 \theta} d\theta = \pi \int_{\pi/4}^{\pi/3} \csc \theta \cot^4 \theta d\theta$

- **[M1]** Transforms integral using substitution.
    

**Step 9: Integrate using a reduction strategy** Let $K = \int \csc \theta \cot^4 \theta d\theta = \int \cot^3 \theta (\csc \theta \cot \theta) d\theta$. By parts: $u = \cot^3 \theta \Rightarrow du = -3\cot^2 \theta \csc^2 \theta d\theta$. $dv = \csc \theta \cot \theta d\theta \Rightarrow v = -\csc \theta$. $K = -\csc \theta \cot^3 \theta - \int (-\csc \theta)(-3\cot^2 \theta \csc^2 \theta) d\theta$ $K = -\csc \theta \cot^3 \theta - 3\int \csc^3 \theta \cot^2 \theta d\theta$

- **[M1]** Attempts integration by parts.
    

Rewrite $\csc^3 \theta \cot^2 \theta$: $\csc^3 \theta \cot^2 \theta = \csc \theta (1 + \cot^2 \theta) \cot^2 \theta = \csc \theta \cot^2 \theta + \csc \theta \cot^4 \theta$ So: $K = -\csc \theta \cot^3 \theta - 3\int \csc \theta \cot^2 \theta d\theta - 3\int \csc \theta \cot^4 \theta d\theta$ $K = -\csc \theta \cot^3 \theta - 3J - 3K$ $4K = -\csc \theta \cot^3 \theta - 3J \Rightarrow K = \frac{1}{4} [ -\csc \theta \cot^3 \theta - 3J ]$

- **[M1, M1]** Splits second integral and correctly identifies $J$ and $K$.
    

**Step 10: Evaluate limits** Evaluate $[-\csc \theta \cot^3 \theta]$ between $\pi/4$ and $\pi/3$: At $\pi/3$: $-\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right)^3 = -\frac{2}{9}$. At $\pi/4$: $-\sqrt{2}(1)^3 = -\sqrt{2}$. Difference: $-\frac{2}{9} - (-\sqrt{2}) = \sqrt{2} - \frac{2}{9}$.

Evaluate $J$ between $\pi/4$ and $\pi/3$: At $\pi/3$: $\frac{1}{2}[\ln(2/\sqrt{3} + 1/\sqrt{3}) - (2/\sqrt{3})(1/\sqrt{3})] = \frac{1}{2}[\ln(\sqrt{3}) - 2/3]$. At $\pi/4$: $\frac{1}{2}[\ln(\sqrt{2} + 1) - \sqrt{2}]$. Difference: $\frac{1}{2}[\ln\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{2}+1}\right) - \frac{2}{3} + \sqrt{2}]$.

**Step 11: Final Volume Calculation** $K = \frac{1}{4} \left[ \sqrt{2} - \frac{2}{9} - \frac{3}{2} \left( \ln\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{2}+1}\right) - \frac{2}{3} + \sqrt{2} \right) \right]$ Expanding and simplifying to the given format yields: $V = \pi \left[ \frac{3}{8}\ln\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{3}}\right) + \frac{7}{36} - \frac{\sqrt{2}}{8} \right]$
