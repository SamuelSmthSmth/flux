### Question 5

**Figure 2** _(A sketch of a rational function crossing the x-axis at x=a. There are vertical asymptotes at x=0 (the y-axis) and x=b, and a horizontal asymptote at y=0 (the x-axis). The curve goes to −∞ as x→0− and +∞ as x→0+. It goes to −∞ as x→b− and +∞ as x→b+.)_

Figure 2 shows a sketch of the curve with equation $y = f(x)$ where $$f(x) = \frac{4(x-1)}{x(x-3)}$$

The curve cuts the x-axis at $(a, 0)$. The lines $y = 0$, $x = 0$ and $x = b$ are asymptotes to the curve.

**(a)** Write down the value of $a$ and the value of $b$. **(2)**

**(b)** On separate axes, sketch the curves with the following equations. On your sketches, you should mark the coordinates of any intersections with the coordinate axes and state the equations of any asymptotes.

(i) $y = f(x+2) - 4$ **(6)** 

(ii) $y = f(|x|) - 3$ **(6)**

### Mark Scheme 5

#### **Part (a)**

**Step 1: Identify roots and asymptotes** $a = 1$ (from the numerator $x - 1 = 0$) $b = 3$ (from the denominator factor $x - 3 = 0$)

- **[B1, B1]** Correct values for $a$ and $b$.
    

#### **Part (b)(i)**

**Step 2: Transformations for $f(x+2) - 4$** This applies a translation of 2 units to the left and 4 units down.

- **[M1]** A horizontal translation (left).
    
- **[M1]** A vertical translation (down).
    

**Step 3: New Asymptotes** Vertical asymptotes move from $x=0, x=3$ to: $x = -2 \quad \text{and} \quad x = 1$ Horizontal asymptote moves from $y=0$ to: $y = -4$

- **[A1ft]** Stated or correctly shown on the graph (ft their $b-2$).
    
- **[B1]** Marks $y = -4$ properly on the graph.
    

**Step 4: New Intersections** Intersection with y-axis ($x=0$): $$y = f(2) - 4 = \frac{4(1)}{2(-1)} - 4 = -2 - 4 = -6$. $(0, -6)$$

- **[B1ft]** $(0, -6)$ correctly marked.
    

Intersection with x-axis ($y=0$): $$f(x+2) - 4 = 0 \Rightarrow \frac{4(x+1)}{(x+2)(x-1)} = 4$$ $$\Rightarrow x + 1 = x^2 + x - 2 \Rightarrow x^2 = 3 \Rightarrow x = \pm\sqrt{3}$$

- **[M1, A1]** Attempts to find P, Q (correct equation) and properly marks them on the graph.
    

#### **Part (b)(ii)**

**Step 5: Transformations for $f(|x|) - 3$** This takes the shape for $x > 0$, mirrors it across the y-axis, and translates it down by 3 units.

- **[M1]** Shape for $x > 0$ crossing x-axis twice.
    
- **[M1]** Shape for $x < 0$ showing reflection symmetry across the y-axis.
    

**Step 6: New Asymptotes** Vertical asymptotes become: $x = \pm 3 \quad \text{and} \quad x = 0$ (implied by the central spike). Horizontal asymptote becomes: $y = -3$

- **[A1ft]** Correct asymptotes shown.
    

**Step 7: New Intersections** Intersection with x-axis ($y=0$) for $x > 0$: $$f(x) - 3 = 0 \Rightarrow \frac{4(x-1)}{x^2 - 3x} = 3$$ $$\Rightarrow 4x - 4 = 3x^2 - 9x \Rightarrow 3x^2 - 13x + 4 = 0$$ $$\Rightarrow (3x - 1)(x - 4) = 0 \Rightarrow x = 4 \text{ or } x = \frac{1}{3}$$

- **[M1, A1]** Forms and solves quadratic for intersections.
    

Using symmetry, the intersections are at: $$x = 4, x = \frac{1}{3}, x = -\frac{1}{3}, x = -4$$

- **[A1ft]** All 4 points must be identifiable on the sketch.
