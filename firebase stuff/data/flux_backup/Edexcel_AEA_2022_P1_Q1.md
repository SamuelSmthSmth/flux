### Question 1

$f(x) = x^{(x^2)} \quad x > 0$

Use logarithms to find the $x$ coordinate of the stationary point of the curve with equation $y = f(x)$. **(5)**

### Mark Scheme 1

**Step 1: Apply logarithms** Writes $f(x) = e^{x^2 \ln x}$ or $$y = x^{(x^2)} \Rightarrow \ln y = x^2 \ln x$$

- **[B1]** Applies $\ln$ (or log) in an appropriate manner.
    

**Step 2: Differentiate** $$f'(x) = \left(2x \ln x + \frac{x^2}{x}\right)e^{x^2 \ln x}$$ or $$\frac{1}{y}\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x} = 2x \ln x + \frac{x^2}{x}$$

- **[M1, A1]** Differentiates $x^2 \ln x$ applying the product rule. Fully correct differentiated expression (both sides) and must be in base $e$, though allow recovery from "log" if base $e$ is implied later.
    

**Step 3: Solve for stationary point** Sets $f'(x) = 0$ or $\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x} = 0$ and solves for $x$. $$f'(x) = 0 \Rightarrow 2x \ln x + x = 0 \Rightarrow \ln x = -\frac{1}{2} \Rightarrow x = \dots$$

- **[dM1]** Solves for $x$ (allow any base).
    

**Step 4: Final Answer** $x = \frac{1}{\sqrt{e}}$ oe

- **[A1]** Correct answer. Accept answers from a correct attempt at differentiating $x^2 \ln x$ even if the "$1/y$" was incorrect. Ignore reference to $x = 0$.
