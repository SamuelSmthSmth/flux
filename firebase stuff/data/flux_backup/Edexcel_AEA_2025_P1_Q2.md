### Question 2

**(a)** On separate diagrams sketch the graph of

(i) $y = |x^2 - 4|$ **(3)**

(ii) $y = ||x^2 - 4| - 5|$ **(4)**

Indicate clearly on each sketch the coordinates of

- any vertices
    
- any points where the graph touches or crosses the coordinate axes
    

**(b)** Hence solve the equation

$$||x^2 - 4| - 5| = \frac{1}{2}x^2$$

**(4)**

### Mark Scheme

#### **Part (a)(i)**

**Step 1: Sketch the basic shape**

- **[B1]** Correct "W" type shape, symmetric about $y$-axis, not below the $x$-axis. Curvature should be correct in each section but condone "almost straight" lines in all but the inverted U shape. Do not be concerned about cusps.
    

**Step 2: Identify turning point**

- **[B1]** Inverted U (condone inverted V) turning point on $y$-axis at $y = 4$. Coordinates not required.
    

**Step 3: Identify $x$-intercepts**

- **[B1]** Correct $x$ intercepts labelled $2$ and $-2$. Coordinates not required. Condone additional $x$ intercepts.
    

#### **Part (a)(ii)**

**Step 4: Sketch the transformed shape**

- **[B1]** Correct shape, symmetric about $y$-axis, not below the $x$-axis. Curvature should be correct in each section but condone "almost straight" lines in all but the U shape. Do not be concerned about cusps.
    

**Step 5: Identify $y$-intercept**

- **[B1]** Correct $y$ intercept at a U (condone V) minimum point on the $y$-axis at $y = 1$. Coordinates not required.
    

**Step 6: Identify $x$-intercepts**

- **[B1]** Correct $x$ intercepts labelled $3$ and $-3$. Coordinates not required. Condone additional $x$ intercepts.
    

**Step 7: Identify other vertices**

- **[B1]** Coordinates of the other two vertices correct and clearly labelled on the diagram. May be seen using $-2$, $2$ and $5$ **clearly** marked on the axes. _(Vertices represent $(-2, 5)$ and $(2, 5))$_.
    

#### **Part (b)**

**Step 8: Set up first quadratic equation**

$$-(x^2 - 4) + 5 = \frac{1}{2}x^2 \rightarrow x = \dots$$ **or** $$(x^2 - 4) - 5 = \frac{1}{2}x^2 \rightarrow x = \dots$$

- **[M1]** Attempts to solve one of the relevant quadratic equations using the correct section of the graph without modulus. May be seen as $9 = \frac{3}{2}x^2$ or $9 = \frac{1}{2}x^2$.
    

**Step 9: Find initial roots**

Two of $x = \pm\sqrt{6}$ and/or $x = \pm3\sqrt{2}$

- **[A1]** Any two correct answers. Accept $\sqrt{18}$ for $3\sqrt{2}$ for this mark.
    

**Step 10: Set up both quadratic equations**

$$-(x^2 - 4) + 5 = \frac{1}{2}x^2 \rightarrow x = \dots$$ **and** $$(x^2 - 4) - 5 = \frac{1}{2}x^2 \rightarrow x = \dots$$

- **[dM1]** Attempts to solve **both** of the relevant quadratic equations using the correct section of the graph without modulus.
    

**Step 11: Final valid roots**

$x = \pm\sqrt{6}$ and $x = \pm3\sqrt{2}$

- **[A1]** All four correct answers and no others. Must now have $3\sqrt{2}$ and not just $\sqrt{18}$. Do not ISW (Ignore Subsequent Working) so e.g. if they discard any of the correct roots, or use an inequality, they score A0.
