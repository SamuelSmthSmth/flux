### **Question 3**

Given that $\phi = \frac{1}{2}(\sqrt{5} + 1)$, **(a)** show that (i) $\phi^{2} = \phi + 1$ (ii) $\frac{1}{\phi} = \phi - 1$ **(4)**

**(b)** The equations of two curves are $$y = \frac{1}{x} \quad x > 0$$ and $$y = \ln x - x + k \quad x > 0$$ where $k$ is a positive constant. The curves touch at the point $P$. Find in terms of $\phi$ (i) the coordinates of $P$, (ii) the value of $k$. **(6)** **(+S1)**

### **Mark Scheme 3**

#### **Part (a)**

**Step 1: Square phi** (i) $$\phi^{2} = \frac{1}{4}(5 + 2\sqrt{5} + 1) = \frac{1}{4}(6 + 2\sqrt{5})$$

- **[M1]** Good attempt at squaring.
    

**Step 2: Complete first proof** $$= \frac{1}{2}(3 + \sqrt{5}) = \frac{1}{2}(1 + \sqrt{5}) + 1 = \phi + 1$$

- **[A1]** All legitimately shown.
    

**Step 3: Rationalize the denominator** (ii) $$\frac{1}{\phi} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{5} + 1} \times \left(\frac{\sqrt{5} - 1}{\sqrt{5} - 1}\right)$$

- **[M1]** Relevant surd work attempted.
    

**Step 4: Complete second proof** $$= \frac{2(\sqrt{5} - 1)}{5 - 1} = \frac{1}{2}(\sqrt{5} - 1) = \frac{1}{2}(\sqrt{5} + 1) - 1 = \phi - 1$$

- **[A1]** All legitimately shown. (Alt: Divide $\phi^2 = \phi + 1$ by $\phi$ to get $\phi = 1 + 1/\phi$).
    

#### **Part (b)**

**Step 5: Differentiate both curves** For the curves to touch, their gradients must be equal. $$\frac{dy_1}{dx} = -\frac{1}{x^2} \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{dy_2}{dx} = \frac{1}{x} - 1$$

- **[M1]** Both gradients attempted.
    

**Step 6: Equate gradients** $$-\frac{1}{x^2} = \frac{1}{x} - 1 \Rightarrow -1 = x - x^2 \Rightarrow x^2 = x + 1$$

- **[M1, A1]** Gradients equated and correct equation deduced.
    

**Step 7: Find P's coordinates** Since $x > 0$, and $x^2 = x + 1$ matches the equation for $\phi$ from (a)(i), $x = \phi$. The y-coordinate is $y = \frac{1}{\phi}$ (or $\phi - 1$). Coordinates of P: $\left(\phi, \frac{1}{\phi}\right)$

- **[A1, A1 (S+)]** Correct x and y coordinates in terms of $\phi$.
    

**Step 8: Find k** Since the curves touch, their y-values are equal at $x = \phi$. $\frac{1}{\phi} = \ln\phi - \phi + k$ $$\Rightarrow k = \frac{1}{\phi} + \phi - \ln\phi$$ Using (a)(ii), this is $$(\phi - 1) + \phi - \ln\phi = 2\phi - 1 - \ln\phi$$ (or $\sqrt{5} - \ln\phi$).

- **[A1]** Accept any sensible equivalent in terms of $\phi$.
    

**Special Mark (+S1)**

- **[S1]** Award S1 for a clear, concise solution, or identifying the correct root of $x^2 = x + 1$ because the other root is negative.
